# Red Flags — Indian Listed Companies

This is the governance and quality lens specific to Indian markets. Most of these can be checked directly from the AR. Run through this whenever you're building a thesis or asked about red flags.

## Promoter-related

**Promoter shareholding direction**
- Falling promoter holding over 2-3 years without explanation = flag. Promoters who believe in the business don't usually sell at scale.
- Buying back / increasing holding = positive signal, but check the route (open market, preferential, warrants).

**Promoter pledging**
- 0-10% pledged: usually fine
- 10-30%: worth noting, ask why
- 30-50%: serious flag, especially if rising
- >50%: assume material risk to minority shareholders if stock falls

**Promoter remuneration vs PAT**
- Promoter-CEO drawing >5% of PAT in compensation = high
- Family members on payroll without clear roles = flag
- Sitting fees / commissions to non-executive promoter directors = check quantum

## Related Party Transactions (RPT)

**Volume**
- RPT as % of revenue: anything >5% deserves scrutiny
- RPT growing faster than revenue over 3 years = flag

**Nature**
- Royalty paid to promoter entity for "brand use": is the brand worth what's being paid? Trajectory of the rate?
- Sales to group entities: check pricing — are these arm's-length?
- Loans given to subsidiaries that never come back, or get converted to equity = flag for capital allocation
- Loans/deposits given to promoter entities = serious flag
- Purchases from promoter entities at above-market rates = serious flag

**Disclosure quality**
- Vague descriptions ("services rendered") without quantum context = flag
- RPT note that's significantly longer than peers = worth a deeper read

## Auditor & disclosure quality

**Auditor changes**
- Big-4 → smaller firm transition without clear reason = flag
- Multiple auditor changes in 3-5 years = serious flag
- Resigning auditors: read the resignation letter (usually filed with stock exchanges)

**CARO 2020 reporting**
The auditor must report on specific items. Things to flag:
- Adverse remarks on fixed assets, inventory verification
- Reporting of fund diversion or end-use deviation for term loans
- Defaults in repayment to banks/FIs/debenture holders
- Loans to parties under common control without proper documentation
- Statutory dues outstanding (PF, ESI, GST, income tax) — even small amounts can signal cash tightness
- Fraud reporting under Section 143(12)

**Audit opinion**
- Qualified, adverse, or disclaimer = serious flag, requires understanding the nature
- Emphasis of matter paragraph = read carefully, often hints at going concern, litigation, or accounting policy concerns
- Key Audit Matters (KAMs) — every audit will have these; check if any KAM has been a recurring item across years (suggests unresolved risk)

## Financial structure red flags

**Working capital**
- Receivable days bloating > revenue growth = sales-stuffing risk or customer quality decline
- Inventory days rising in a non-seasonal pattern = demand softening or obsolescence risk
- Sudden improvement in working capital days that's hard to explain = possibly window-dressing

**Debt**
- Short-term debt rising as % of total debt = refinancing risk
- Foreign currency debt without natural hedge = currency risk
- Inter-corporate deposits placed with related parties = capital being diverted from operations

**Cash flow vs profit**
- Profit growing but operating cash flow flat or declining over 3 years = working capital is absorbing cash, or earnings quality is weak
- High dependence on "other income" for profit = check the source (interest on inter-corporate deposits? gains on investment sale?)

**Capex quality**
- Capex announcements without follow-through = "vision capex" used to drive narrative
- Capex done but no commensurate revenue/margin pickup after the gestation period = sub-optimal capital allocation

## Subsidiary structure

- Material foreign subs in tax-friendly jurisdictions (Mauritius, Singapore, Cyprus) without operating substance = governance risk
- Frequent changes in subsidiary structure (mergers, demergers, spinoffs) = check the rationale, often value is being moved around in ways that don't help minorities
- Loss-making subsidiaries continuously funded by the parent = capital allocation question
- Holding company structure with multiple listed entities = holding co discount risk, plus governance complexity

## Contingent liabilities

Indian companies routinely carry meaningful contingent liabs from tax disputes (income tax, GST, customs). Things to flag:
- CL as % of net worth: anything >25% is material
- Trajectory: rising contingent liab over 3-5 years = pattern of disputes
- Specific disputes that could crystallize: read the note carefully, look for items that have lost at multiple appellate stages

## ESOPs & dilution

- Repeated large ESOP issuances at deep discounts to market = wealth transfer concern
- Promoter family receiving ESOPs (vs only employees) = flag
- ESOP exercise prices that look unusually low = check if there was a special grant policy

## Sector-specific things to check

**NBFC / Banks**
- Stage 3 (NPA) trajectory, restructured book, write-offs ratio
- Provisioning coverage
- Funding mix (CASA for banks, borrowing mix for NBFCs)
- Capital adequacy headroom

**Real estate**
- Inventory days (units unsold + WIP)
- Customer advances (refundable deposits)
- Pre-sales vs revenue recognition gap
- Contingent liab on customer disputes (RERA cases)

**Pharma**
- USFDA inspection outcomes (483s, warning letters)
- Concentration in single product / single customer
- R&D capitalization vs expensing policy

**IT services**
- Top 5 / Top 10 client concentration
- Onsite-offshore mix
- Attrition trajectory
- Sub-contracting cost trend (sometimes hides margin pressure)

## How to write the red flags section

Don't dump the full checklist. Run through it mentally, then surface only what's material for this specific company. For each flag worth mentioning:
1. State the specific issue (with the actual number / fact from the filings)
2. Quantify materiality (is this 0.5% of revenue or 15%?)
3. Trajectory (one-time, or pattern over years?)
4. What it would take to resolve / what to watch

A red flags section that lists 12 generic concerns is less useful than one that names 3 specific, material issues.
