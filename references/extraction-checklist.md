# Extraction Checklist — What to pull from each document type

The goal here is to be systematic so nothing important gets missed. Don't dump everything — extract, then synthesize in the master report.

## Annual Report (AR)

The AR is the densest document. Sections that matter most:

### Director's Report / MD&A
- Chairman's letter — tone, what they emphasize, what they deflect
- Macro context they're framing the business in (any defensiveness?)
- Strategic priorities listed for the year ahead — are these consistent with what was said last year, or has the story shifted?
- Any major announcements: new capex, new geography, new product, new acquisition

### Segment reporting (mandatory note)
- Revenue by segment, last 2-3 years
- EBIT/PBIT by segment
- Segment capital employed if disclosed
- Geography mix if disclosed separately
- Calculate (qualitatively) which segment is growing fastest, most profitable, most capital-intensive

### Related Party Transactions (RPT) note
- Total RPT as % of revenue and as % of profit
- Who are the related parties (group companies, promoter entities, KMP)
- Nature of transactions: sales, purchases, loans given/taken, royalty, brand fees
- Any unusual items: large royalty paid to promoter entity, loans to subsidiaries that look like equity in disguise, sales to group entities at uncertain pricing
- Trajectory: is RPT quantum growing faster than revenue?

### Contingent liabilities note
- Tax disputes (income tax, GST, customs, excise) — quantum, status
- Bank guarantees, performance guarantees
- Legal claims against the company
- Any contingencies that look like they could crystallize

### Auditor's Report + CARO annexure
- Type of audit opinion (clean, qualified, adverse, disclaimer) — qualified or worse is a serious flag
- Key Audit Matters (KAMs) — what did the auditor identify as significant risk areas?
- Emphasis of matter paragraphs
- CARO 2020 reporting on: fixed assets verification, inventory verification, loans to related parties, deposits, statutory dues, term loan utilization, fund diversion, fraud reporting, internal audit
- Auditor changes in last 3 years — and reasons given

### Subsidiary list (Form AOC-1 or equivalent)
- How many subs, where domiciled
- Material subs (>10% of consolidated revenue or PAT)
- Loss-making subs — direction over time
- Foreign subs in tax-friendly jurisdictions — what's their actual operating substance?

### Promoter shareholding & pledging (from BSE/NSE filings, sometimes referenced in AR)
- Promoter holding %, trajectory
- Pledged shares as % of promoter holding — anything >20% is worth flagging, >50% is a serious risk
- Any institutional investors in top 10 — quality of holders matters

### ESOPs / managerial remuneration
- ESOP pool size and vesting pattern
- Top management remuneration (especially promoter-CEO) vs PAT — anything >5% of PAT is high
- Variable vs fixed split

### Capex & cash flow
- Capex done in the year vs announced last year
- Free cash flow — is the company self-funding growth, or are debt/equity raises filling the gap?

## Quarterly Results (QR)

Much shorter. Focus on:
- Revenue and growth (YoY, QoQ) — and growth in volume vs price if separable
- EBITDA margin trajectory
- Segment performance if disclosed
- Any one-offs called out (forex, tax adjustment, asset sale, exceptional items)
- Debt levels (gross and net)
- Working capital signals if disclosed (receivables, inventory)

## Investor Presentation

These are often the cleanest summary of what management wants you to take away. Useful for:
- Segment-wise revenue/margin charts
- Forward guidance, capex roadmap
- Order book / capacity utilization (sector-dependent)
- Industry positioning slides
- ESG/sustainability metrics if relevant

But: remember this is a sales document. Cross-reference everything against the actual financials.

## Concall Transcript

The Q&A section is more valuable than the prepared remarks.

### From prepared remarks
- Forward guidance: revenue growth, margin, capex
- New initiatives announced
- Any framing of macro / demand environment
- Capital allocation priorities stated

### From Q&A
- Which analysts asked the toughest questions, and how were they handled?
- Was any question deflected, met with vague language, or answered with a non-answer?
- Did management commit to specific numbers or stay vague?
- Any visible discomfort — long pauses, re-routing to CFO, "we'll come back to that"
- Consistency check: does this concall's commentary line up with last quarter's? If they sounded bullish on demand last quarter and cautious now, that's a real signal
- Tone on margins: language like "moderation", "near-term pressure", "input cost headwinds" usually means the next quarter will print weaker than expected
- Capital allocation specificity: "we're evaluating M&A" is vague; "we've earmarked Rs X for capex over Y years across these projects" is concrete
